Raw2Ready

Overview

Built at the Cursor AI Hackathon in Hamburg, Raw2Ready is an AI-based business intelligence website that turns a rough business idea into pitch-ready assets — a business model breakdown, competitor analysis, a timeline, a pitch presentation and a portfolio video — coded almost entirely inside Cursor over a 48-hour sprint with a 4-person team.

Problem

Having a rough idea is easy; making it legible to an investor isn't. It's hard to visualize an early idea, and the information that does exist usually ends up scattered across a long ChatGPT conversation. Turning that into something pitch-ready still means separately producing a polished presentation and a video — a slow, manual step most early founders skip or outsource.

Approach

Raw2Ready takes a raw idea — name, location, budget, industry and a free-text description — and runs it through a pipeline that produces a business model, a competitor analysis, a timeline, a pitch presentation and a portfolio video, one stage feeding the next. The backend lets the user pick between two LLM providers per request and returns structured output (competitors, market estimates, target demographics, service extensions) that drives both the on-screen results and the generated deck.

My Contribution

This was built as part of a 4-person team over a 2-day hackathon. My contributions:

  • Built the FastAPI backend that receives the raw idea and routes it to the selected LLM provider.
  • Integrated both OpenAI (GPT-5.2) and Google Gemini (2.5 Flash) behind a single analysis endpoint, including the REST fallback used when the official Gemini SDK proved unstable.
  • Designed the prompts that turn unstructured idea input into the structured JSON (competitors, market estimate, target demographics, service extensions) consumed by the frontend and the deck builder.
  • Implemented the AI video generation stage, calling the MiniMax video API to produce the portfolio video from the generated business analysis.

Results

Shipped a working end-to-end prototype within the 48-hour window — from raw idea input to generated competitor and market analysis, an auto-built pitch deck, and an AI-generated portfolio video. The team didn't place, but came away with a functioning full-stack AI product and a lot learned about building fast, in Cursor, under hackathon time pressure.

Challenges / Learnings

  • Coordinating a 4-person team to design and integrate a full-stack app in 48 hours.
  • Normalizing responses from two different LLM providers (OpenAI and Gemini) behind one API.
  • Working around instability in the official Gemini SDK by falling back to direct REST calls.
  • Getting AI video generation working reliably under tight hackathon time constraints.
